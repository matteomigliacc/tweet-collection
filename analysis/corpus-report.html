Diagnosis & fixes

1 · Error-spike casualties — fix by re-scraping (— tweets)

Seven party accounts were scraped 23:10–00:20 while X’s backend threw Dependency/Timeout errors (~700 warnings, peaking at 209 per 10 min). Each errored search query silently returned 0 tweets and the month was checkpointed as done — 100% of zero-result months were immediately preceded by a warning. The tweets are still on X: reset checkpoints for these handles and re-run at a calm hour.

2 · Structural search gaps — solved by fetching each tweet by ID (— tweets)

X’s search index does not return old @-replies once they fall beyond the ~3,200-tweet Replies-tab reach. No amount of re-scraping reaches them, and narrowing the query window does not help either — a single-day search of a reply-heavy account still returns only what the index holds.

This bucket is empty as of 20 Jul, and the reasoning behind it was wrong. The conclusion here used to be that these tweets could only be injected from the professors’ JSON, on the evidence that Segers was checkpoint-reset, re-scraped from scratch in a run with zero backend errors, and came back with the same —-tweet hole. That evidence was real but the inference was too broad. Search cannot reach these tweets; tweet_details can. Asking X for all 1,834 Segers IDs directly returned 1,687 tweets plus 157 thread-mates, with zero deleted — 75.6% → 100%. Nothing was injected: the tweets were live on X the whole time, just not searchable. Recovered tweets carry source='by_id' so provenance stays auditable.
Hugo de Jonge left this bucket earlier the same day. A re-scrape recovered 196 of his 197 missing tweets — 190 from June 2020 alone, a single month that had come back empty and been checkpointed done anyway. Between the two cases the diagnostic is: a hole concentrated in one or two months is a checkpointing failure and re-scrapes away; a hole spread evenly across years is the search index, and that one needs fetching by ID.

3 · Minor scatter (— tweets across everyone else)

Since-deleted tweets, edit-history ID changes, and a handful of search misses, spread thin over every remaining handle. Not worth chasing individually; most would come along free with the two fixes above. Handles with nothing missing are omitted here.

Where the corpus is ahead

The reference scrapes stop at their run date and lean on the same search index we do, so our Replies-tab pass and later runs pick up tweets they never had. These are net additions to the research corpus, listed largest first.
